# Tracking & Trajectory Kinematics for Aerial Traffic Video — Research Notes

Date: 2026-07-29. Scope: MOT algorithm choice, trajectory smoothing standards, velocity-measurement fusion, stop/wave detection, physical scale, and visualization prior art — for the `tva` library (4K drone clips of highway jams, per-frame homography to world plane, CA-Kalman + RTS smoothing, stop-onset extraction, space-time diagrams, backward wave-speed measurement).

---

## 1. MOT algorithm choice for aerial vehicles

**The short version: our current ByteTrack is fine as a base; BoT-SORT is what recent UAV-traffic pipelines actually pick, mostly for its camera-motion compensation (GMC) — which our per-frame homographies already largely provide. Appearance/ReID trackers (StrongSORT, Deep OC-SORT) are aimed at pedestrian occlusion benchmarks and buy little in nadir aerial views where occlusion is rare.**

- **ByteTrack** (Zhang et al., ECCV 2022): Kalman + Hungarian, two-stage association — high-confidence detections first, then low-confidence detections matched to leftover tracks by IoU. This second stage is exactly what recovers vehicles whose detection confidence flickers in dense queues (partial shadows, tight packing). Ultralytics default. https://trackers.roboflow.com/latest/trackers/bytetrack/
- **BoT-SORT** (Aharon et al., 2022): ByteTrack backbone + (a) camera-motion compensation via sparse optical flow / ECC applied to KF predictions, (b) KF state that tracks width/height directly, (c) optional ReID with IoU-appearance fusion. Roboflow's head-to-head benchmark page: "BoT-SORT is the choice when camera ego-motion is strong and you need the most stable identities… reduces ID switches on panning or handheld footage." https://trackers.roboflow.com/latest/trackers/comparison/ and https://trackers.roboflow.com/develop/trackers/botsort/
- **OC-SORT** (Cao et al., CVPR 2023): observation-centric re-update — on re-association it back-fills the virtual trajectory from observations rather than trusting accumulated KF prediction error. Wins when motion is non-linear or tracks are lost during turns/pans. For highway stop-and-go the relevant OC-SORT insight is that a constant-velocity KF accumulates error while a target is unobserved *and decelerating*; observation-centric re-update fixes the re-acquisition jump. (Comparison summary on the Roboflow page above.)
- **StrongSORT** (Du et al., 2023): DeepSORT modernized + two *offline* modules — AFLink (appearance-free tracklet linking) and **GSI (Gaussian-process smoothed interpolation)** to fill detection gaps. SOTA MOT17/MOT20 at publication. The offline modules are the transferable idea for us (we are an offline pipeline). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.13514
- **Deep OC-SORT** (Maggiolino et al., ICIP 2023): OC-SORT + adaptive-weight ReID; 63.9 HOTA on MOT20 (rank 1 at the time), strongest under heavy occlusion/crossover (DanceTrack). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.11813

**What UAV-traffic papers actually use:**
- The Songdo georeferenced-trajectory pipeline (Fonod et al., "Advanced computer vision for extracting georeferenced vehicle trajectories from drone imagery," arXiv 2024) **adopted BoT-SORT** explicitly "for its enhanced Kalman filter state vector and efficient EMC [motion compensation] … effective in handling sporadic misdetections," as an upgrade over ByteTrack. https://arxiv.org/html/2411.02136
- UAV target-following study (MDPI Drones 8(9):488, 2024): BoT-SORT held target IDs where ByteTrack showed "a substantial tracking performance drop… from the lack of camera movement compensation." https://www.mdpi.com/2504-446X/8/9/488
- A 2026 UAV MOT benchmark ("Breaking Smooth-Motion Assumptions") evaluates StrongSORT, BoT-SORT, and Deep OC-SORT with standard ReID models on aggressive-motion UAV footage — i.e., appearance models are used when the *camera* motion breaks the motion model. https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05970v1
- pNEUMA's own extraction (Barmpounakis & Geroliminis 2020) and the visual-extension work (Kim et al. 2023) treat tracking noise as a *post-processing/denoising* problem rather than chasing tracker SOTA — consistent with our smoothing-heavy design. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0968090X19310320 , https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0968090X22003795

**ID switches in dense stop-and-go, practically:** in near-nadir 4K footage occlusion is minimal; ID switches come from (a) detector flicker on small/packed vehicles → ByteTrack's low-confidence stage already targets this; (b) camera drift/gimbal wobble → GMC or our homography stabilization; (c) long detection dropouts on stopped vehicles → fix offline with tracklet stitching (a stopped vehicle's world position barely moves, so gating by world-plane distance + time gap is near-unambiguous) plus GSI-style gap interpolation. Tuning knobs that matter for stop-and-go: raise `track_buffer` to ~2 s of frames (stopped cars can be missed for many frames without moving), keep `match_thresh` (IoU) tight since neighbors are close, and turn on BoT-SORT GMC only if we ever track in raw pixel space instead of the stabilized world plane.

## 2. Trajectory smoothing standards (highD, pNEUMA, NGSIM reconstruction)

**Our CA-Kalman + RTS with adaptive measurement noise is exactly the published standard — highD uses the identical recipe. What we should add from the literature is (a) kinematic-plausibility QA metrics (jerk analysis, internal consistency), and (b) outlier rejection *before* the smoother.**

- **highD** (Krajewski et al., ITSC 2018): "Using Rauch-Tung-Striebel (RTS) Smoothing and a **constant acceleration model**, the trajectory of each vehicle was refined taking into account all detections. This improved the positioning error to a pixel size level." Claimed positioning error typically < 10 cm from 4K drone video at ~100 m altitude. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.05642 , https://levelxdata.com/highd-dataset/
- **inD** (Bock et al., 2020): same — Bayesian smoothing, constant-acceleration model, detections replaced by interpolation where erroneous. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.07602
- **Montanino & Punzo** (TRB 2013 "Making NGSIM data usable"; TR-B 2015 reconstruction + validation): the canonical multistep reconstruction — (1) remove outliers/impossible points, (2) cut physically implausible speed/acceleration spikes, (3) reconstruct to restore **kinematic consistency** (position must equal integrated speed; acceleration bounded), (4) final low-pass smoothing. Applied to NGSIM I80-1: resulting data "show physically consistent distributions of acceleration, maximum speed and minimum inter-vehicle spacing, **unlike the measured ones**." https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191261515001393 , https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC76757
- **Punzo, Borzacchiello & Ciuffo** (TR-C 2011): the QA methodology — **jerk analysis** (fraction of |jerk| above a physical bound, e.g. 15 m/s³, should be ~0), **consistency analysis** (re-integrate speed and compare to measured displacement; drift = bias), **spectral analysis** (noise floor visible as flat high-frequency plateau). NGSIM famously failed all three raw. Adopt these three as automated pipeline QA. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0968090X10001701
- **Coifman & Li** (TR-B 2017): manual re-extraction showed NGSIM's smoothed data still contained large position errors — smoothing hides but does not remove systematic extraction error; the detector/homography stage matters as much as the filter. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261517300838
- **Adaptive EM-RTS for UAV video** (Measurement, 2026): estimates *time-varying* measurement noise via EM inside the RTS loop — the published version of our "adaptive measurement noise"; reported trajectory-consistency gains 70.6% → 81.3%. Direct validation of our design. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0263224126023626
- **Smoothing-technique bake-offs on UAV data** (Springer 2025/2026, DataFromSky trajectories): compare moving average, symmetric EMA, Kalman, Savitzky-Golay (and up to nine methods) on **three criteria: position accuracy, internal consistency, physical realism** — no single winner, but state-space (Kalman/RTS) methods win on consistency because speed/acceleration are *states*, not derivatives. https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-1037-2_21 , https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41062-026-02522-3
- **pNEUMA extensions** (Kim, Anagnostopoulos, Barmpounakis, Geroliminis, TR-C 2023): penalized/regularized denoising because no ground truth exists; anomaly detection to flag human-induced extraction errors. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0968090X22003795
- **Wavelets** in this literature are used for *event localization* (see §4, Zheng et al.), not for differentiation. Savitzky-Golay is the common non-Bayesian alternative for derivatives but has no mechanism for varying noise or gaps.

**Key principle for speed/acceleration:** never finite-difference smoothed positions; take v and a directly as states of the CA RTS smoother (what highD/inD do). Reported accuracy anchors: highD < 10 cm position ("pixel-level"); with sub-decimeter position at 25-30 fps and RTS, speed RMSE lands in the ~0.05-0.2 m/s range (drone datasets rarely publish speed RMSE because no ground truth exists — pNEUMA-class satellite work reports ~3.4 m/s against GPS, which is the *bad* end: https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14698v1 ; well-calibrated fixed-camera systems on BrnoCompSpeed reach mean speed error < 1-2 km/h ≈ 0.3-0.6 m/s, see §5).

## 3. Position + velocity fusion (optical flow as a direct velocity measurement)

**Nobody in the drone-trajectory-dataset world does this — they are position-only + RTS. But per-vehicle LK flow as a direct speed measurement is standard in adjacent fields (UAV ego-velocity navigation, fixed-camera speed estimation), and the Kalman fusion math is textbook. It is a genuine differentiator for us, and it is especially good at exactly our two goals: precise speed curves and crisp stop onsets (flow is *identically zero* for a stopped car, with no dependence on bbox-center jitter).**

- Fixed-camera vehicle speed via sparse LK flow: Sangsuwan et al., IEEE Access 2024 ("Video-Based Vehicle Speed Estimation Using Lucas-Kanade…", 34 citations) https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel7/6287639/10380310/10381710.pdf ; earlier: Harris corners + LK per vehicle, IEEE 2014 https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6960689 ; LK for tracking + velocity, IEEE 2023 https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10165227 . Practitioner repos use the same recipe (ROI + lane-scale calibration + LK): https://github.com/swhan0329/vehicle_speed_estimation
- UAV navigation fuses LK optical-flow velocity with a Kalman filter as a matter of course (Miller et al. 2019, optical-flow videonavigation, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6471049/ ; SRCKF flow-velocity fusion, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35104989/ ). Same measurement model, camera pointed the other way.
- Why bbox differencing is worse: detection-center jitter (±1-3 px frame-to-frame) differentiates into large velocity noise at 30 fps; flow correlates textured patches across frames directly, so its velocity noise is set by sub-pixel matching (~0.1-0.3 px) instead of detector head instability.

**Concrete recipe (recommended):** per track per frame, take Shi-Tomasi corners inside the bbox eroded ~20-25% (avoid background pixels at edges), pyramidal LK to the next frame, **map both endpoints through their frames' homographies**, take the per-corner world displacement, use the **median** (robust to corners that latched onto road texture or neighbor cars), divide by dt → z_v. Feed as a velocity measurement into the CA Kalman alongside the position measurement, with its own R_v; gate it (Mahalanobis) so degenerate flow (motion blur, textureless roofs, glare) is dropped. Mapping through per-frame homographies automatically subtracts drone ego-motion — the equivalent of GMC. Cheap validation: on manually-verified stopped vehicles, z_v should read < 0.05 m/s.

## 4. Stop detection, queue dynamics, shockwave speed

### Stop / wave definitions
- **Critical-speed wave definition** (Ji et al., "Scalable analysis of stop-and-go waves," TR-C 2026 / arXiv 2409.00326, on I-24 MOTION): a stop-and-go wave = a connected spatio-temporal region where vehicle speed < a chosen critical speed; wave **front** points = where each trajectory drops below the threshold, wave **tail** points = where it recovers; waves become connected components in a graph, which also captures bifurcation/merging. This is the modern, scalable formalization — and it maps 1:1 onto our stop-onset events. Gallery: https://trafficwaves.github.io/ ; paper: https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00326v2 , https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0968090X25003894
- Practical thresholds in the queue literature: agency queue detection uses speed-below-threshold (e.g., 15 mph) sustained at a sensor (FHWA queue guidance, https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/63565/dot_63565_DS1.pdf ). For trajectories, "stopped" is typically v < 0.5-1 m/s sustained; our hysteresis classifier (stop below ~0.3-0.5 m/s, release above ~1-1.5 m/s, with dwell times) is the standard debouncing and needs no change — it is a per-vehicle version of Ji's critical-speed crossing with two thresholds.
- **Stop-onset localization without differentiation:** Zheng, Ahn, Chen & Laval (TR-B 45(9), 2011) use the **Mexican-hat wavelet transform of each vehicle's speed series**; the wavelet-energy peak marks the deceleration-wave arrival at that vehicle, robust to noise. They used it to trace oscillation formation/propagation vehicle-by-vehicle across 53 cases. This is the best published method for "pinpoint the stop onset." https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261511000622 (companion bottleneck paper: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261510001037 )
- **Edie's generalized definitions** (Edie 1963, "Discussion of traffic stream measurements and definitions," 2nd Int. Symp. on the Theory of Traffic Flow): flow q = total distance traveled / area of a space-time region, density k = total time spent / area, v = q/k. The correct way to compute macroscopic fields from our trajectories in space-time bins (then the wave is a structure in that field). Referenced throughout the wave literature above; textbook treatment in the TU Delft OCW chapters below.

### Wave-speed values (the "famous 15-20 km/h")
- **Sugiyama et al. 2008** (New Journal of Physics 10, 033001 — the circular-road experiment, the canonical phantom-jam citation): "The jam cluster is clearly observed and it moves **backward at a velocity of roughly 20 km/h**," maintaining its size, while free vehicles move ~40 km/h. https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1367-2630/10/3/033001
- **Ji et al. 2026** (I-24 MOTION, thousands of waves): waves propagate upstream at a "relatively constant speed of **10-20 km/h**." https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0968090X25003894
- **Treiber & Helbing's Adaptive Smoothing Method** (2002/2011): reconstructs speed fields by smoothing along characteristics, using congested-wave speed **c_cong ≈ -15 km/h** as a universal constant of congested traffic. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/1838303_An_Adaptive_Smoothing_Method_for_Traffic_State_Identification_from_Incomplete_Information
- **TU Delft OCW shockwave chapters** (Knoop/Hoogendoorn course notes): worked stop-and-go examples with backward wave speeds of ~16 km/h; terminology: **stop wave** (backward-forming shockwave) vs **start wave** (backward-recovery wave). https://ocw.tudelft.nl/wp-content/uploads/Chapter-5-Shock-wave-theory.pdf , https://ocw.tudelft.nl/wp-content/uploads/Chapter-8.-Shock-wave-analysis.pdf
- **Newell 2002** ("A simplified car-following theory: a lower order model," TR-B 36): in congestion, follower trajectory = leader trajectory shifted by (τ, -δ); the wave speed is w = δ/τ, constant — the microscopic basis for why the wave slope is straight in the space-time diagram. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261500000448 ; empirical verification: Ahn, Cassidy & Laval, TR-B 2004, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191261503000742
- Theory of why the wave self-sustains: **jamitons** — Flynn, Kasimov, Rosales, Seibold (Phys. Rev. E 79, 056113, 2009), traveling-wave solutions analogous to detonation waves, consistent with Sugiyama's experiment. https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevE.79.056113 , https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.2828

### Wave-speed estimators from trajectories (what we should implement)
1. **Stop-onset regression** (primary): collect (t_i, x_i) stop-onset events (hysteresis crossing or wavelet-energy peak) belonging to one wave (cluster by proximity in space-time, or Ji-style connected component below critical speed); robust-fit a line (Theil-Sen or RANSAC — onsets from lane changes are outliers); slope = wave speed, report with bootstrap CI. This is wave-front fitting exactly as Ji et al. formalize it.
2. **Speed-field characteristic slope** (secondary, whole-field): Edie-bin the trajectories into a (t, x) speed field; find the orientation that maximizes autocorrelation (equivalently Radon transform peak, or Treiber-ASM-style: the c that maximizes smoothed-field fit). Uses all data, not just onsets; good cross-check.
3. **Newell pairwise shift** (microscopic cross-check): for consecutive same-lane vehicle pairs through the wave, find (τ, δ) minimizing mismatch between leader and shifted follower speed profiles; w = δ/τ per pair; the distribution's median should agree with 1 and 2.

Expect 4-6 m/s (14-22 km/h); if our estimate falls outside ~10-25 km/h backward, suspect scale or timing errors before believing it.

## 5. Physical scale: car-length vs lane-width priors

- **Car-length prior:** used by the auto-calibration line of work — Dubská, Herout & Sochor (BMVC 2014, "Automatic Camera Calibration for Traffic Understanding") recover scene scale from statistics of known mean vehicle dimensions; https://bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/2014/files/paper013.pdf . Fleet mean passenger-car length ≈ 4.4-4.7 m, but individual vehicles range ~3.6 m (compacts) to 5.8 m (crew-cab pickups) — a single-vehicle prior carries ~8-10% error; averaging over N detected cars shrinks it ~1/√N (≈1-2% with 50+ cars), and classifying out trucks first is mandatory (US semi ≈ 21-22 m). Note highD ships measured per-vehicle length/width, so its distributions can serve as the prior: https://levelxdata.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/highD-Format.pdf
- **Lane-width prior:** US interstate standard lane = 12 ft (3.66 m) with very low variance on mainline freeways — a tighter prior than one car length, and measurable many times along the frame; the practitioner recipe lists lane width (~3.5 m) as the first-choice reference (https://github.com/swhan0329/vehicle_speed_estimation ). Caveats: paint-center vs paint-edge ambiguity (~0.1-0.15 m on 3.66 m ≈ 3-4%), and auxiliary/ramp lanes deviate.
- **Accuracy ceiling:** **BrnoCompSpeed** (Sochor et al., IEEE T-ITS 2017/2018; 20,865 vehicles with LiDAR-gate ground-truth speeds) shows calibration/scale is the dominant error source in visual speed measurement; the best calibrations get mean speed error to ~1-2 km/h (~2-3%), and the follow-up vanishing-point method cut scale error by 50% vs prior SOTA. https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.06441 , https://www.researchgate.net/publication/313879450
- **Our situation is better than either prior:** with a homography to a georeferenced orthophoto (state DOT orthoimagery / high-res basemap with known GSD), scale is set by surveyed ground control, typically <1% — that should be the primary scale source, with lane-width (3.66 m) and mean-car-length (4.5 m over many cars) as independent sanity checks. The Songdo pipeline (arXiv 2411.02136) is the model here: georeference first, priors as QA.

## 6. Storytelling / visualization prior art

- **The canonical footage:** Sugiyama's circular-road video (supplementary to the NJP paper, endlessly re-embedded) — the visual grammar of "wave moves backward while cars move forward" started here. https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1367-2630/10/3/033001
- **Explainers:** TED-Ed "What is phantom traffic?" (https://www.facebook.com/TEDEducation/videos/872506259921151/ ), NOVA PBS "The hidden cause of traffic jams" (https://www.facebook.com/NOVApbs/videos/1627808741511669/ ), CGP Grey "The Simple Solution to Traffic" (chicken-snake metaphor, ring-road animation). Common device: top-down animated cars + one highlighted car, then reveal the aggregate wave.
- **Interactive essays:** "Phantom: how traffic jams form out of nothing" (Pablo Williams) — scrolling space-time canvas where "backward-leaning streaks are the jam waves," with a draggable cursor linked to a live ring simulation; the best current example of coupling a space-time diagram to an animation. https://pablowilliams.github.io/Phantom/ ; ring-road jam simulators: https://github.com/MoReEbrahimi/traffic-jam-simulation ; Treiber's traffic-simulation.de is the long-standing academic interactive.
- **Research-grade conventions:** I-24 MOTION / Ji et al. space-time diagrams — time on x, distance (mile marker) on y, speed as color (viridis or green→red), waves appear as dark diagonal bands; their wave-topology gallery is a ready-made reference for how measured waves look at scale: https://trafficwaves.github.io/ . TU Delft OCW chapters use trajectory-line diagrams with shockwave lines overlaid and labeled (stop wave / start wave) — the right template for an annotated "here is the wave, slope = X km/h" figure. - **For our overlays:** draw all trajectories as thin low-alpha lines colored by speed; highlight one followed car as a bold accent line in both the video overlay and the space-time diagram simultaneously (same color); annotate the fitted wave line with a slope wedge labeled "wave: ~18 km/h backward"; mark stop-onset dots where trajectories cross the critical speed — those dots visually *are* the wave front.

## Recommended kinematics stack

**Tracker.** Stay tracking-by-detection with ultralytics. Prefer **BoT-SORT config** (`botsort.yaml`) with GMC off if detections are already homography-stabilized into the world plane (our homographies are better GMC than sparse-flow GMC); ReID off unless observed ID-switch rate demands it. Stop-and-go tuning: `track_buffer` ≈ 2 s of frames, `track_low_thresh` ≈ 0.1 (keep ByteTrack's low-conf recovery), `new_track_thresh` ≈ 0.6, IoU `match_thresh` ≈ 0.8. Add an **offline post-pass**: stitch tracklets gated by world-plane distance + time gap (stopped cars barely move — near-unambiguous), then let the RTS smoother interpolate gaps (StrongSORT's GSI role).

**Measurement model (position + velocity fusion).** Per frame per track, two measurements into one CA Kalman (state [x, y, vx, vy, ax, ay], process noise on jerk):
1. z_p = homography-mapped box centroid, adaptive R_p (innovation/EM-based, per the adaptive EM-RTS paper — keep our current mechanism);
2. z_v = median world-plane displacement of pyramidal-LK-tracked Shi-Tomasi corners inside the eroded bbox, endpoints mapped through their own frames' homographies (auto-cancels drone motion), own R_v, Mahalanobis-gated.

**Smoother.** Forward KF + **RTS backward pass, constant-acceleration model** — unchanged; this is literally the highD recipe. Take v and a from the smoothed state, never by differencing. Add Montanino-Punzo-style pre-filtering (outlier removal, spike cutting) *before* the smoother, and adopt **Punzo 2011 QA**: jerk analysis (share of |jerk| > 15 m/s³ ≈ 0), internal consistency (∫v vs x drift), spectral check — as automated pipeline tests.

**Stop events.** Keep hysteresis: stopped when v < 0.3-0.5 m/s sustained ≥ 1 s; moving when v > 1.0-1.5 m/s sustained ≥ 0.5 s. Define **stop onset** as the wavelet-energy peak (Mexican hat on the speed series, Zheng et al. 2011) or, simpler, the last downward crossing of the critical speed before the stop — the critical-speed crossing is also exactly Ji et al.'s wave-front point, so events and waves share one definition.

**Wave speed.** Three estimators, cross-validated: (1) **Theil-Sen/RANSAC line through stop-onset (t, x) points** per wave (primary, matches wave-front formalism); (2) **Radon/autocorrelation slope of the Edie-binned speed field** (whole-field cross-check, ASM-style); (3) **Newell pairwise (τ, δ) fits**, w = δ/τ (microscopic cross-check). Sanity band: 10-25 km/h backward (Sugiyama ≈ 20; I-24 ≈ 10-20; ASM prior 15).

**Scale.** Primary: homography to georeferenced ortho (sub-1%). QA priors: lane width 3.66 m (US interstate), fleet-mean car length ≈ 4.5 m averaged over ≥ 50 classified cars (~1-2%). A wave speed outside the sanity band is first a scale bug.
